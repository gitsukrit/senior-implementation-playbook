# The Scenario

A mid-size organization runs 50+ workflow variants across four operational departments. The environment has three properties that make this hard:

**Legacy infrastructure.** The system of record is a Microsoft Access database with manual paper routing for approvals. The IT team maintains it, is protective of it, and is resistant to changes they didn't initiate.

**Fixed political deadline.** Leadership has committed to a 9-month go-live. That commitment was made publicly and cannot be quietly moved.

**Three competing departments.** Each department believes their workflows should be configured first. One department head has direct access to executive leadership and has already made informal commitments to the vendor's sales team — commitments that don't fully match the signed statement of work.

Two additional dynamics shape the engagement:

The customer PM is organized and process-oriented but has a pattern of characterizing verbal conversations as decisions when it's convenient. Undocumented decisions accumulate and become disputes later.

A public-facing data integration is required at go-live. The IT contractor supporting the receiving system has provided limited documentation and no clear timeline. A senior executive has already publicly promised this integration will be live on Day 1.

The engagement is being staffed by a Solutions Architect (SA) working alongside a customer-facing PM. The SA is responsible for architecture, phasing, test strategy, integration risk, and decision governance — well enough documented that the PM can manage the client without the SA in every session.

## Why this scenario is worth thinking about

This isn't a scenario about technology. It's a scenario about **navigating regulated implementation work under political pressure**.

Every framework in this playbook came out of reasoning through this specific problem. But the patterns generalize — the same shape shows up in healthcare payer implementations, financial services compliance projects, utility modernization work, public administration technology rollouts, and any regulated environment where multiple stakeholders with legitimate authority disagree about priorities under a fixed timeline.

Substitute the specifics. The patterns hold.
